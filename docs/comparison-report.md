# Comparison report: PINNs against classical methods

What this project measured, phase by phase: the accuracy of each PINN against an exact solution
or the AERO-01 panel method, what it cost to train, how many steps it took to converge, and
where it failed. Every number comes from `validation/results/` and is regenerated by
[`validation/run_all.py`](../validation/run_all.py). Column definitions are in
[`validation/results/README.md`](../validation/results/README.md).

Hardware for every timing: NVIDIA RTX 2060 (6 GB), clock-capped at 1000 MHz; i7-10750H for the
panel method. PyTorch 2.15, float32 training, float64 references.

Total GPU training time: Phase 1 36 min (12 runs), Phase 2 44 min (6), Phase 3 73 min (43),
Phase 5 53 min (5); Phase 4 trains nothing. Errors: Phase 1 velocity on a 64 × 128 polar grid;
Phases 2–3 velocity on 8 validation sections; Phase 5 the state $(\rho, u, v, p)$.

Two patterns hold across phases:

- **L-BFGS does the converging.** In every phase the error drops sharply when L-BFGS takes over
  from Adam. The Phase 2–3 runs reached 1e-2, if at all, only in the L-BFGS stage.
- **Smooth problems kept improving until the optimiser stopped; shocks did not.** The smooth
  cases either ran to the end of their budget still improving, or (most Phase 1 runs) stopped
  because L-BFGS met its own tolerance at errors near 1e-4. The three wedge cases stalled near
  5 % error, 1000–1200 steps before their budget ended.

## 3. PINN against the panel method

**Accuracy.** On the cylinder, the hard-constraint PINN's velocity error (7.7e-5) is ten times
smaller than a 512-panel solution's (9.3e-4 on r ≥ 1.05), because the panel method converges
at first order on a curved body. On airfoils the order reverses. The family PINN's lift error,
about 0.03, matches a 12–16-panel solution, while 200 panels give 5e-4.

**Cost per query.** One 200-panel lift costs 8.0 ms on the CPU. The PINN costs 154 µs per case
in batches of 10⁴ on the GPU, 85 % of it building the contours, or 66 µs from the circulation.
A single query costs the PINN 8.9 ms on the GPU and 3.7 ms on the CPU.

**Break-even.** Charging the 657 s of training, the PINN beats the 200-panel solver after
84,000 lift evaluations, or 391,000 counting the architecture search. It beats the 16-panel
solver, which is as accurate as the PINN, only after 2.0–2.8 million.

**Design loops.** For $c_l = 0.8$ on six sections, Newton with autograd derivatives took 4
batched evaluations (0.064 s) against Brent's 36 panel solves (0.27 s); the PINN's incidences
were 0.05–0.41° off. A 41 × 41 lift carpet took 0.26 s against 13.7 s, 0.030 off on average.

## 4. Failure cases

Every failure is reported in its lesson with evidence. In short:

| Phase | Failure | Evidence | Cause and remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soft-constraint ψ stalls at 1e-3 | needs 5600 steps to 1e-2, hard needs 400 | loss imbalance between residual and boundary terms; hard constraints remove it |
| 2 | Nine formulations failed before the final one | Lesson 2, Section 3: zero circulation, leaks through the tail, a hidden far-field doublet, a slowed-down flow | directional Kutta condition, tangency term, far-field derivative penalty, residual scaling, and above all inverse-Joukowski input features |
| 2 | Lift slope about 20 % low (circulation lift) | test cl error 0.121 | physics alone under-predicts lift; the Phase 3 data weight cut it to 0.037 |
| 2 | Cp spikes at the trailing edge | max abs ΔCp 1.29 on the test set | the sharp edge, where the flow is singular; RMS away from the edges is 0.05 |
| 2 | Outside the family box | lift off by 0.04–0.37 on NACA 0021, 2406, 8412 and 2412 at 12° | a surrogate does not extrapolate |
| 3 | The proxy overstated the gain | 2.4× better at the proxy budget, 1.6× at the full one | short budgets favour fast starters; retrain the winner |
| 4 | The speed-up is small at equal accuracy | 2.5× against 16 panels | a 2D panel method is about the cheapest solver there is |
| 5 | Shocks | 4.6–5.6 % error; shock smeared over 0.13–0.17 of the domain, 0.9–2.4° too shallow | a smooth network cannot represent a jump; artificial viscosity and reweighting moved the error by about a percentage point |

## 5. Reproducibility

- `uv run python validation/run_all.py` retrains everything, about 4–5 GPU-hours. With
  `--quick` it smoke-tests every script in 3 minutes; with `--plots-only` it redraws from the
  saved results.
- Trained networks are in `validation/results/checkpoints/` (≤ 82 kB each). The notebooks load
  them, run on one CPU thread and rebuild byte-identically.
- Seeds are fixed. GPU training is not bit-reproducible (CUDA reductions), so a rerun matches to
  the seed spread, not exactly.
- Each budget row records its git commit. The `-dirty` flags on Phases 1–3 and 5 are explained
  in the results README; none marks uncommitted model code.
- 89 tests, each against a closed-form result or a cited value, run in about 35 s on the CPU.
